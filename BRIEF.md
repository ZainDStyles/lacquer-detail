# HALO Motorworks — scroll website brief

## What the reference reel does (and my verdict)
A tuning-shop site ("Hexline"): blue Porsche 911 under hexagon ceiling lights.
Shutter opens → car drives in → onto the lift → brake swap → exhaust swap → dyno → night drive.
A "build sheet" box in the corner updates the specs as you scroll (443 hp → 580 hp).

**What works:** clear story (stock → built), one signature light (the hex ceiling), the live build sheet, short punchy captions.
**What's weak:**
- Lots of people and hands. AI hands and faces wobble, and you can see it.
- Too many separate clips. Each cut changes the room or car slightly.
- The white smoke flash is used to hide the joins, and it reads as a cheat.

**So our version:** keep the story and the live build sheet. Use **no people**. Use **fewer, longer clips**. Do the part swaps in **code**: a laser-scan line wipes from the stock part to the new part on the *same* photo, so they line up perfectly.

## The look
- **Brand:** HALO Motorworks, a performance tuning studio
- **Signature light:** a ceiling of glowing **ring (halo) LED lights**, our answer to their hexagons
- **Car:** **Nissan GT-R R35** in **Katsura Orange** (deep metallic orange). It's a tuning legend, and Stage 2 numbers sell themselves.
- **Upgrade colour / site accent:** **electric cyan**: cyan brake calipers, cyan scan line, cyan HUD
- **Studio:** matte black walls, polished dark-grey epoxy floor with reflections, black roller shutter, blue two-post lift, tool chests

## The rule for perfect images (the reference chain)
1. **Master image first:** the GT-R parked inside the studio. Every later image uses it as the reference, so the car and the room stay the same.
2. Every other image is an **edit of the master** ("same car, same room, only change X").
3. Video clips use those exact images as **start and end frames**.
4. Phone versions are made as 9:16 images from the same references.
